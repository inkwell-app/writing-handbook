# Completed example: a library workshop decision

All names, numbers, source notes and events below are fictional teaching material. They are not evidence about an actual library, survey or Inkwell customer.

## Brief

A community library must choose between one longer writing workshop and two shorter sessions. The coordinator needs a one-page decision note by Friday. Success means a named choice, a reason, the limits of the evidence and a check after the pilot. The note should not turn a small convenience sample into a claim about all members.

## Baseline paragraph

> Members prefer short workshops. We should replace the Saturday workshop with two evening sessions. This will double attendance and make the program accessible to everyone.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Available source note | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | Fictional sign-in sheet: 12 attended the last Saturday workshop; 4 left before the final exercise | Some participants did not stay for the full session | Why they left, or how another schedule would perform |
| E02 | Fictional follow-up: 6 of 8 respondents selected a 45-minute option | Six respondents favored a shorter option | The preferences of nonrespondents or all library members |
| E03 | Fictional room calendar: two evening slots are available next month | The pilot is possible on those dates | Demand, accessibility or attendance |

## Feedback and revision decisions

| ID | Feedback | Decision | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | “Say attendance will double; two sessions means twice the capacity.” | Declined: capacity is not attendance | No attendance forecast remains |
| R02 | “Keep the Saturday workshop; the sample is small.” | Partly accepted: preserve the option while piloting evenings | Recommendation is limited to one month |
| R03 | “Name what would make us stop the pilot.” | Partly accepted; scheduling remains open | Review questions appear below; the coordinator still needs to set the date |

## Revised paragraph

> Pilot two 45-minute evening workshops next month, while retaining the following month’s Saturday booking. Six of eight respondents to our fictional follow-up selected the shorter option; this is a small, self-selected sample, so it does not establish a preference across the membership. The room calendar supports two trial dates. After the pilot, the coordinator will compare attendance, completion of the final exercise and participant feedback with the previous workshop, record differences in topic and promotion, and decide whether to repeat, adjust or stop the evening format.

## Check the dependent claims

The recommendation now distinguishes a reversible pilot from a permanent change. `E02` supports the duration choice, not the claim that evenings are more accessible. The revised paragraph therefore makes no universal accessibility claim. The review names measurements without inventing a numerical success threshold the team never agreed on.

## Handoff manifest

- Source: `workshop-decision-02.md`.
- Reader: the library coordinator; purpose: approve or revise the pilot.
- Export: PDF for the meeting; Markdown for subsequent edits.
- Verification: open the PDF, confirm the evidence limits and open scheduling decision remain visible, and check that the source notes travel with the editable file.
- Open decision: the coordinator must set the review date and agree on how participant feedback is collected before scheduling.

Try an alternative recommendation using the same evidence. What additional information would justify making the change permanent? Add that question to the evidence ledger rather than manufacturing a stronger conclusion.
